# LightSem: 15 Offline Body-to-N-Light Algorithms

The current mapper is structurally incapable of preserving much morphology: it samples roughly ten joints, maps 3D body state into effectively two target coordinates, creates exact cross-type target duplicates, and forces persistent brightness. Low angular error is therefore mostly measuring mechanical obedience to a lossy intent—not meaningful body-to-light transfer.

All 15 candidates below are **deterministic offline mappings from the approved saved body array only**. They use no training, audio encoder, copied body, autonomous temporal oscillator, beat chase, random motion, or source-time warp. A frozen body must produce constant intent, constant solved motion, and constant brightness.

## Shared contract

Let:

- $$P_t \in \mathbb{R}^{22\times3}$$: body joints at time $$t$$, at 40 Hz after deterministic resampling.
- $$G=(V,E)$$: fixed native skeleton graph.
- $$o_i$$: origin of fixture $$i$$.
- $$N$$: number of outputs.
- $$q_{t,i}$$: desired world target for fixture $$i$$.
- $$a_{t,i}\in$$: scalar intensity; RGB remains supplied externally and must pass byte-for-byte unchanged.[0][1]
- $$R(\theta_{t,i})$$: world direction generated by pan/tilt state $$\theta_{t,i}$$.

Every mapper should output an **intent** object, then pass it to the existing mechanics compiler:

$$
\min_{\theta}\sum_{t,i} w_{t,i}
\, d_\angle\!\left(R(\theta_{t,i}),q_{t,i}-o_i\right)^2
+\lambda_v \|D\theta\|^2
+\lambda_a \|D^2\theta\|^2
$$

subject to pan, tilt, velocity, acceleration, octagonal derivative constraints, quantization margin, and DMX encoding. Time indices never move; only spatial amplitude may be reduced.

A constant pose must satisfy:

$$
P_t=P_0\quad \forall t
\Longrightarrow
q_{t,i}=q_{0,i},\quad a_{t,i}=a_{0,i}.
$$

## 1. Anatomical end-effector rays

**Paper.** *Neural Kinematic Networks for Unsupervised Motion Retargetting* (2018), Ruben Villegas, Jimei Yang, Duygu Ceylan, Honglak Lee. arXiv:1804.05653. The paper uses a forward-kinematics layer to evaluate motion on a target skeleton with different bone lengths.[1]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Explicitly evaluate target morphology through kinematics.

**LightSem adaptation.** Direct named-body transfer. Use head, sternum, pelvis, wrists, elbows, knees, ankles, and toes as explicit source effectors. Do not cycle fixtures through four chains.

For source effector $$e$$, use a root-frame displacement:

$$
d_e(t)=R_{\text{root}}(t)^T
\left(P_e(t)-P_{\text{pelvis}}(t)\right).
$$

Map it injectively to a bounded 3D stage region:

$$
q_e(t)=c_e+
\begin{bmatrix}
s_x\tanh(d_{e,x}/r_x)\\
s_y\tanh(d_{e,y}/r_y)\\
s_z\tanh(d_{e,z}/r_z)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

For multiple fixtures manifesting one effector:

$$
q_{e,k}(t)=q_e(t)+\rho_k n_e(t),
$$

where $$n_e$$ is an instantaneous anatomical plane normal from neighboring bones and $$\rho_k$$ is a fixed spatial offset, never a time phase.

Intensity:

$$
a_{e,k}(t)=
\operatorname{clip}\left(
a_{\text{floor}}+
c_v\tanh\left(
\frac{\|\dot P_e(t)\|}{v_0}
\right),0,1\right).
$$

Use $$a_{\text{floor}}=0$$ as the primary experiment; retain 0.28 only as a historical-baseline ablation.

**N scaling.** Allocate fixtures deterministically across named effectors. Extra fixtures create simultaneous local fans, rings, or parallel manifestations of that same effector.

**Freeze behavior.** Fixed body position gives fixed $$d_e$$, fixed $$n_e$$, fixed targets, and zero speed-derived change in brightness.

**Targets.** Missing head response; ten-joint undersampling; exact beam/laser desired-target duplication.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Head perturbation gain.
- Per-joint finite-difference response.
- Desired-target duplicate fraction.
- Source-effector-extremum to emitted-ray-extremum recall.
- Body joint coverage.

**Pitfall.** Literal effectors may look like puppetry rather than composition. Tanh saturation can still erase large gestures if spatial scales are too small.

## 2. Bone-frame angular fans

**Paper.** *Neural Kinematic Networks for Unsupervised Motion Retargetting* (2018), Villegas et al., arXiv:1804.05653.[1]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Skeleton motion can be represented by rotations and bone orientations rather than only positions.

**LightSem adaptation.** Transfer bone direction and joint bend rather than endpoint position.

For directed bone $$j=(p\rightarrow j)$$:

$$
b_j(t)=\frac{P_j(t)-P_p(t)}
{\|P_j(t)-P_p(t)\|+\epsilon}.
$$

For an articulated joint:

$$
\beta_j(t)=
\arccos\left(
b_{\text{parent}(j)}(t)\cdot b_j(t)
\right).
$$

Define an anatomical bend normal:

$$
n_j(t)=
\frac{
b_{\text{parent}(j)}(t)\times b_j(t)
}{
\|b_{\text{parent}(j)}(t)\times b_j(t)\|+\epsilon
}.
$$

Then fixture $$k$$ assigned to this bone gets:

$$
d_{j,k}(t)=
\operatorname{normalize}
\left(
B_i b_j(t)+\rho_k B_i n_j(t)
\right),
$$

where $$B_i$$ is a fixed body-to-stage coordinate basis for fixture $$i$$.

Intensity:

$$
a_{j,k}(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
c_\beta \beta_j(t)+
c_\omega \|\dot b_j(t)\|,
0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** Assign fixture groups to bones. Additional fixtures sample wider or denser simultaneous fans in the instantaneous joint-bend plane.

**Freeze behavior.** Fixed pose gives fixed bone directions, bend values, fan directions, and intensity.

**Targets.** Current position-map nullspace; elbow/knee morphology disappearing despite substantial angular articulation.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Bone-angle-to-emitted-angle coherence.
- Signed bend-extremum recall.
- Ratio of source versus emitted angular spectral RMS in 1–2, 2–4, and 4–8 Hz bands.
- Sensitivity to perturbations orthogonal to each bone.

**Pitfall.** Straight limbs make the bend normal numerically unstable. Use a deterministic geometric fallback axis tied to the body frame, but freeze it exactly during holds.

## 3. Primal-skeleton branch allocation

**Paper.** *Skeleton-Aware Networks for Deep Motion Retargeting* (2020), Kfir Aberman, Peizhuo Li, Dani Lischinski, Olga Sorkine-Hornung, Daniel Cohen-Or, Baoquan Chen. arXiv:2005.05732. The paper reduces homeomorphic skeletons to a shared primal skeleton using degree-2 chain pooling.[2]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Topology-aware reduction of degree-2 chains into meaningful skeletal branches.

**LightSem adaptation.** Treat limbs, spine/head, and appendages as branches with endpoint and curvature information. This explicitly includes spine and head rather than ignoring them.

For branch $$m=(j_0,\dots,j_L)$$, let $$p_m(t,s)$$ be a piecewise linear interpolation along the chain. Define:

$$
c_m(t)=P_{j_L}(t)-P_{j_0}(t)
$$

and branch curvature residual:

$$
\kappa_m(t,s)=
p_m(t,s)-
\left[(1-s)P_{j_0}(t)+sP_{j_L}(t)\right].
$$

For fixture $$k$$ at branch fraction $$s_k$$:

$$
q_{m,k}(t)=
F_m(c_m(t))+
H_m \kappa_m(t,s_k).
$$

Intensity:

$$
a_{m,k}(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
c_1\|\dot c_m(t)\|+
c_2\|\partial_t \kappa_m(t,s_k)\|,
0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** Allocate outputs to branches by deterministic branch importance:

$$
w_m=
\text{arcLength}_m\cdot
\operatorname{Var}_{t,s}\left(p_m(t,s)\right).
$$

Use largest-remainder rounding to distribute $$N$$ outputs. More outputs sample branch curvature more densely; they do not add independent temporal behavior.

**Freeze behavior.** Constant branches imply constant chord, curvature, targets, and brightness.

**Targets.** The current mapper’s repeated chain sampling, sparse joint coverage, and complete omission of head/spine.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Fraction of joints with nonzero emitted-output Jacobian.
- Branch curvature reconstruction error.
- Target effective dimension.
- Per-branch light allocation.
- Duplicate desired-target rate.

**Pitfall.** Topology preserves structure but not necessarily camera-space visibility. A rigid straight branch can consume outputs with little visible differentiation.

## 4. Analytic pose-aware body-part pooling

**Paper.** *Pose-aware Attention Network for Flexible Motion Retargeting by Body Part* (2023), Lei Hu, Zihao Zhang, Chongyang Zhong, Boyuan Jiang, Shihong Xia. arXiv:2306.08006. PAN treats body parts as the transfer units and computes pose-dependent joint weights before pooling.[3]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Dynamic body-part pooling.

**LightSem adaptation.** Replace learned attention with transparent analytic weights. Parts: head/torso, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg.

For joint $$j$$ in body part $$m$$:

$$
u_j(t)=
\alpha\|P_j(t)-P_{\text{anchor}(m)}(t)\|
+\beta\|\dot P_j(t)\|
+\gamma\beta_j(t).
$$

$$
w_j(t)=
\frac{\exp(u_j(t))}
{\sum_{h\in J_m}\exp(u_h(t))}.
$$

Pooled point:

$$
z_m(t)=\sum_{j\in J_m}w_j(t)P_j(t).
$$

Pooled covariance:

$$
\Sigma_m(t)=
\sum_{j\in J_m}w_j(t)
(P_j-z_m)(P_j-z_m)^T.
$$

The first principal direction of $$\Sigma_m$$ provides a part axis. Targets can use both:

$$
q_{m,k}(t)=
F_m(z_m(t))+
\rho_k v_{\max}(\Sigma_m(t)).
$$

Intensity:

$$
a_{m,k}(t)=
\sum_{j\in J_m}w_j(t)
\operatorname{clip}(\|\dot P_j(t)\|/v_0,0,1).
$$

**N scaling.** Split fixtures by part, then distribute additional outputs across covariance-axis samples or fixed body-part local offsets.

**Freeze behavior.** Every term is instantaneous; constant pose means constant softmax weights, pooled point, covariance, target, and brightness.

**Targets.** Fixed chain cycling that cannot adapt when a different joint becomes salient.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Attention entropy by body part.
- Fraction of time each joint receives meaningful attribution.
- Head/torso contribution.
- Target dimension.
- Extremum preservation after mechanics.

**Pitfall.** Near ties can make softmax attribution unstable. Cap logits and use no causal smoothing. If filtering is necessary, use a symmetric whole-path filter that maps a constant to itself exactly.

## 5. Normalized relational-distance constellations

**Paper.** *Skinned Motion Retargeting with Residual Perception of Motion Semantics & Geometry* (R2ET, 2023), Jiaxu Zhang et al., arXiv:2303.08658. R2ET uses normalized pairwise joint distances to capture motion semantics while separately addressing shape geometry.[4]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Pairwise normalized distance representation.

**LightSem adaptation.** Build a small landmark constellation that preserves body-wide relational shape rather than individual limbs alone.

Choose landmarks $$S$$, e.g. head, sternum, pelvis, wrists, elbows, knees, ankles, toes. Normalize by median bone length:

$$
D_{ab}(t)=
\frac{\|P_a(t)-P_b(t)\|}
{\operatorname{median}_{(u,v)\in E}\|P_u(t)-P_v(t)\|}.
$$

Solve for stage constellation points $$X_a(t)$$:

$$
\min_X
\sum_{a<b}
w_{ab}
\left(
\|X_a-X_b\|-s_{ab}D_{ab}
\right)^2
+
\lambda
\sum_a\|X_a-X_a^0\|^2.
$$

Use fixed asymmetric anchors $$X_a^0$$ to prevent reflection ambiguity.

Fixtures target landmark positions, relation midpoints, or fixed fractions on relation segments:

$$
q_{ab,k}(t)=
(1-\eta_k)X_a(t)+\eta_k X_b(t).
$$

Intensity:

$$
a_{ab,k}(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
\frac{|\dot D_{ab}(t)|}{d_0},
0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** Add fixtures by sampling more relation edges and fixed fractions of those edges. The relation graph is declared before tests.

**Freeze behavior.** Constant pair distances give a constant optimization problem and a canonical fixed solution.

**Targets.** 3D-to-2D nullspace; failure to represent torso-to-limb, hand-to-head, and cross-body shape.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Pair-distance distortion after intent generation.
- Pair-distance-extremum recall after emitted DMX.
- Procrustes-aligned constellation error.
- Joint-response coverage.
- Actual visible-hit separation.

**Pitfall.** Pure distances lose chirality. You need signed volume or oriented-area terms, otherwise left/right reflections can map to the same constellation.

## 6. Contact-graph bridges

**Paper.** *Contact-Aware Retargeting of Skinned Motion* (2021), Ruben Villegas, Duygu Ceylan, Aaron Hertzmann, Jimei Yang, Jun Saito. arXiv:2109.07431. It identifies self- and ground-contact relationships and preserves them during retargeting.[5]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Contact is semantic motion information, not merely geometry noise.

**LightSem adaptation.** Define continuous proximity fields between semantically relevant joint pairs. Do not trigger a flash at a threshold crossing.

For pair $$(a,b)$$:

$$
c_{ab}(t)=
\exp\left[
-\left(
\frac{\|P_a(t)-P_b(t)\|}
{\sigma_{ab}}
\right)^2
\right].
$$

Midpoint and pair axis:

$$
m_{ab}(t)=\frac{P_a(t)+P_b(t)}{2},
\qquad
v_{ab}(t)=P_a(t)-P_b(t).
$$

Use a “bridge” formation:

$$
q_{ab,k}(t)=
F\left(
(1-\eta_k)P_a(t)+\eta_kP_b(t)
\right),
\qquad \eta_k\in.[0][1]
$$

Intensity:

$$
a_{ab,k}(t)=
c_{ab}(t)\,
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
\frac{\|\dot v_{ab}(t)\|}{v_0},
0,1
\right).
$$

Optionally retain a constant low value during sustained contact, but it must be an explicit control, not a hidden fade state.

**N scaling.** More fixtures densify the same instantaneous bridge or add predefined semantic pair types: wrist–wrist, wrist–head, wrist–torso, toe–floor proxy, wrist–pelvis.

**Freeze behavior.** Contact/proximity and geometry remain fixed under a frozen pose.

**Targets.** Clap-like gestures, hand-to-head gestures, crossed-arm forms, feet/ground-like stability, onset and release semantics.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Correlation between source $$c_{ab}(t)$$ and emitted bridge occupancy.
- Contact onset/offset timing error.
- False contact-light rate.
- Contact relation extrema recall.

**Pitfall.** Sparse joints are not dense body surfaces. A wrist near a torso joint is only a proxy for body contact, so this must not be advertised as true collision preservation.

## 7. Sparse observer-frame interaction field

**Paper.** *Skinned Motion Retargeting with Dense Geometric Interaction Perception* (MeshRet, 2024), Zijie Ye et al., arXiv:2410.20986. MeshRet uses semantically consistent mesh sensors and local-frame dense interaction features to preserve contact and non-contact body geometry.[6]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Express relative interaction in a local observer frame.

**LightSem adaptation.** Use sparse joint sensors only. This is explicitly **not** MeshRet’s dense mesh field.

For sensor joint $$a$$, construct a local frame:

$$
F_a(t)=
\begin{bmatrix}
b_a(t) & n_a(t) & b_a(t)\times n_a(t)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

For another joint $$b$$:

$$
h_{ab}(t)=
F_a(t)^T(P_b(t)-P_a(t)).
$$

The components of $$h_{ab}$$ become stage coordinates:

$$
q_{ab}(t)=
c+
\begin{bmatrix}
s_x\tanh(h_{ab,x}/r_x)\\
s_y\tanh(h_{ab,y}/r_y)\\
s_z\tanh(h_{ab,z}/r_z)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Intensity:

$$
a_{ab}(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
\frac{\|\dot h_{ab}(t)\|}{h_0},
0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** Rank sensor pairs once using full-path variance and anatomical coverage; allocate outputs to pairs by that fixed rank. More fixtures represent fixed spatial samples of the same pair feature.

**Freeze behavior.** Fixed local frames and relative coordinates produce constant outputs.

**Targets.** Global coordinate mappings that miss “hand relative to chest,” “head relative to shoulder,” or “wrist around torso” morphology.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Local-frame feature preservation.
- Response to hand-around-torso perturbations.
- Pair coverage.
- Camera-space target diversity.
- Frame-flip count.

**Pitfall.** Local frames become unstable for nearly straight or collinear body segments. A deterministic sign convention is required; it must not introduce time-dependent drift in a hold.

## 8. Body-stage Laplacian interaction mesh

**Paper.** *OmniRetarget: Interaction-Preserving Data Generation for Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation and Scene Interaction* (2025), Lujie Yang et al., arXiv:2509.26633. It uses an interaction mesh and Laplacian-coordinate preservation under target constraints.[7]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Differential coordinates preserve local relational structure relative to a scene.

**LightSem adaptation.** Build one fixed interaction graph across source body landmarks and stage anchors: floor points, wall points, body-screen bounds, centerline landmarks.

For graph weights $$w_{ij}$$, define:

$$
L_i(P)=
P_i-\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}w_{ij}P_j.
$$

Solve stage manifestation vertices $$X(t)$$:

$$
\min_X
\sum_i
\left\|
L_i(X)-A_iL_i(P)
\right\|^2
+
\lambda_A\|X_{\text{anchors}}-\bar X_{\text{anchors}}\|^2
+
\lambda_B C_{\text{bounds}}(X).
$$

Fixtures point at selected vertices or edges:

$$
q_{i,k}(t)=
(1-\eta_k)X_u(t)+\eta_kX_v(t).
$$

Intensity is local differential deformation speed:

$$
a_i(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
\frac{\|\partial_tL_i(P_t)\|}{\ell_0},
0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** Add outputs by sampling fixed graph edges or triangular/tetrahedral interpolation points. Graph topology remains fixed for the complete sequence.

**Freeze behavior.** Constant body Laplacian coordinates imply a constant linear system and constant stage geometry.

**Targets.** Fixed lanes; mismatch between intended target spacing and actual visible stage footprint; lack of body-to-floor/wall relational composition.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Laplacian distortion.
- Camera projected occupancy.
- Floor versus wall usage.
- Actual hit-pair distance.
- Ray-pair crossing count.
- Body-screen overlap.

**Pitfall.** A bad static interaction graph can encode irrelevant relations. Recomputing Delaunay topology per frame is dangerous because topology changes are themselves unrequested choreography.

## 9. Shape-context silhouette transfer

**Paper.** *Shape Matching and Object Recognition Using Shape Contexts* (2002), Serge Belongie, Jitendra Malik, Jan Puzicha. Shape contexts encode the distribution of other points around each point using log-polar histograms and solve correspondences through assignment.[8]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Relational point-set descriptors and correspondence.

**LightSem adaptation.** Treat selected body landmarks as a projected body silhouette and fixture hit points as a stage point set.

For projected landmark positions $$u_a$$, construct a shape context:

$$
H_a(r,\phi)=
\sum_{b\neq a}
\mathbf{1}
\left[
(\log\|u_b-u_a\|,\angle(u_b-u_a))
\in \text{bin}(r,\phi)
\right].
$$

For fixture hit candidates $$y_i$$, build equivalent descriptors $$\hat H_i$$. Define correspondence cost:

$$
C_{a,i}=
\chi^2(H_a,\hat H_i)
+
\lambda \|u_a-y_i\|^2.
$$

Solve assignment with Hungarian matching or entropic relaxation.

**N scaling.** With $$N>|\text{landmarks}|$$, create fixed descriptor-bin samples around each matched landmark. More fixtures increase point-set density, not temporal motion.

**Freeze behavior.** A fixed projected silhouette produces identical descriptors and deterministic assignments.

**Targets.** Lane saturation; loss of whole-body outline; body configurations that differ relationally but look identical under the current projection.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Shape-context distortion.
- Occupied log-polar bins.
- Left/right chirality error.
- Projected footprint area.
- Hold leakage.

**Pitfall.** A frontal silhouette loses depth. Add a second sagittal projection or a signed depth channel; this is still a shape descriptor, not a full 3D body model.

## 10. Entropic optimal-transport formation

**Paper.** *Sinkhorn Distances: Lightspeed Computation of Optimal Transportation Distances* (2013), Marco Cuturi. arXiv:1306.0895. It computes entropy-regularized optimal transport efficiently using Sinkhorn-Knopp matrix scaling.[9]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Continuous, mass-preserving assignment rather than brittle one-to-one mapping.

**LightSem adaptation.** Treat body features as gesture mass and reachable stage cells as demand locations.

Create body tokens $$x_a(t)$$, e.g. joints, bone midpoints, relation midpoints. Define nonnegative gesture mass:

$$
\mu_a(t)=
\frac{
\epsilon+
\|\dot x_a(t)\|
}{
\sum_b[\epsilon+\|\dot x_b(t)\|]
}.
$$

Create fixed stage cells $$y_b$$ with capacities $$\nu_b$$. Cost:

$$
C_{ab}(t)=
\|F(x_a(t))-y_b\|^2+
\lambda_{\text{mech}}
C_{\text{reachable}}(i,y_b).
$$

Solve:

$$
\Gamma^\star=
\arg\min_{\Gamma\mathbf 1=\mu,\,
\Gamma^T\mathbf 1=\nu}
\langle \Gamma,C\rangle+
\varepsilon
\sum_{a,b}
\Gamma_{ab}(\log\Gamma_{ab}-1).
$$

For fixture $$i$$, target a barycenter of cells assigned to its semantic token:

$$
q_i(t)=
\frac{\sum_b\Gamma^\star_{a(i),b}(t)y_b}
{\sum_b\Gamma^\star_{a(i),b}(t)}.
$$

Intensity is transported mass:

$$
a_i(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
c_m\sum_b\Gamma^\star_{a(i),b}(t),0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** Increase stage-cell resolution and allocate more fixtures to transported mass regions. Mass is divided; extra fixtures do not create new gesture energy.

**Freeze behavior.** Constant body tokens and masses produce a constant transport plan.

**Targets.** Output collapse; poor stage coverage; inability to scale from 8 to 100+ fixtures without repeated lanes.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- OT cost.
- Stage-cell occupancy.
- Mass conservation.
- Pairwise actual-hit distribution.
- Freeze-output variation.
- Source-token attribution.

**Pitfall.** High entropy blurs gestures; low entropy produces unstable quasi-discrete assignments. $$\varepsilon$$ must be frozen before comparing songs.

## 11. Signed relational-stress formation

**Paper inspiration.** R2ET supplies normalized relational distances; OmniRetarget supplies constrained relational optimization ideas.[4][7]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Preserve pairwise relation structure under target constraints.

**LightSem adaptation.** Map directed body relations to a screen/stage formation while retaining chirality.

For relation $$(a,b)$$:

$$
d_{ab}(t)=
\frac{\|P_a-P_b\|}{\ell_{\text{body}}},
$$

and a signed orientation:

$$
\sigma_{ab}(t)=
\operatorname{sign}
\left[
\left(
(P_a-P_{\text{pelvis}})
\times
(P_b-P_{\text{pelvis}})
\right)\cdot \hat y
\right].
$$

Let $$Y_i$$ be actual intended hit points. Solve:

$$
\min_Y
\sum_{(i,k)}
w_{ik}
\left(
\|Y_i-Y_k\|-\hat d_{ik}
\right)^2
+
\lambda_s
\operatorname{softplus}
\left(
-\sigma_{ik}\,
\operatorname{Area}(Y_i,Y_k,c)
\right).
$$

The first term preserves desired relation lengths. The second prevents mirrored layouts.

Intensity:

$$
a_{ab}(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
c_d|\dot d_{ab}(t)|
+c_\sigma|\partial_t \tilde\sigma_{ab}(t)|,
0,1
\right),
$$

where $$\tilde\sigma$$ is a continuous signed-area surrogate, not the discontinuous sign alone.

**N scaling.** Add relation edges or fixed edge subdivisions. The graph remains declared and fixed for both songs.

**Freeze behavior.** Constant relation lengths and signed areas yield a fixed formation.

**Targets.** Opposite-lean erasure from rotated-neutral subtraction; mirror ambiguity; different angle streams producing visually similar patterns.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Signed relation preservation.
- Formation stress.
- Projected ray crossings.
- Actual hit-pair distances.
- Left/right mirror error.

**Pitfall.** Nonlinear stress minimization has local minima. Use deterministic initialization and fixed asymmetric anchors; never rely on random restarts unless seeds are fixed and logged.

## 12. Laban Effort–Shape field

**Papers.** *Affective Movement Generation using Laban Effort and Shape and Hidden Markov Models* (2020), Ali Samadani, Rob Gorbet, Dana Kulic, arXiv:2006.06071. It uses Laban Effort and Shape abstractions for expressive movement representation.  *LaMoGen: Laban Movement-Guided Diffusion for Text-to-Motion Generation* (2025), arXiv:2509.24469, uses quantitative motion features such as energy, acceleration, jerk, and body volume.[10][11]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Quantified expressive motion descriptors.

**Not borrowed.** No HMM generation, no diffusion, no text-to-motion model, no learned style transfer.

**LightSem adaptation.** Separate body-wide Shape from Effort.

Define body volume proxy:

$$
V(t)=
\operatorname{vol}
\left(
\operatorname{ConvHull}
\{P_j(t)\}_{j\in S}
\right),
$$

kinetic-energy proxy:

$$
E(t)=
\sum_j m_j\|\dot P_j(t)\|^2,
$$

acceleration:

$$
A(t)=
\sum_j m_j\|\ddot P_j(t)\|,
$$

and jerk:

$$
J(t)=
\sum_j m_j\|\dddot P_j(t)\|.
$$

Use Shape to control stage formation scale and anisotropy:

$$
q_i(t)=
c+
S(V(t),\text{diagonal}(t))q_i^0+
B_i(P_t).
$$

Use Effort for scalar intensity:

$$
a_i(t)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
w_E\tilde E(t)+
w_A\tilde A(t)+
w_J\tilde J(t),
0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** All outputs sample a fixed formation template whose spatial scale and deformation are body-dependent at that instant.

**Freeze behavior.** Static pose gives constant volume and zero derivatives, therefore constant formation and constant configured static intensity.

**Targets.** Persistent visual clutter; no macro expansion/contraction; weak phrase-level physical expression.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Correlation between body volume and emitted footprint area.
- Correlation between Effort features and total visible optical energy.
- Dimmer-duty distribution.
- Low-motion luminous duty.
- Extrema/onset timing.

**Pitfall.** Global descriptors can conceal which body part caused the event. Always store joint-level contributions alongside the aggregate.

## 13. Deterministic motif-recurrence staging

**Paper inspiration.** Samadani et al. compare movements in Laban descriptor space before building a generative abstraction.  Shape Contexts provide descriptor-based shape similarity and matching.[8][10]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Similar body configurations can be recognized in descriptor space.

**LightSem adaptation.** Repeated source-body motifs should produce recognizably related stage layouts. This is not an HMM, no generated state sequence, and no time-indexed choreography.

Build descriptor:

$$
z(t)=
[
D_{ab}(t),
\beta_j(t),
V(t),
E(t),
\text{signed orientation features}
].
$$

Use deterministic segmentation at body-derived extrema/onsets. Fit $$K$$-medoids using a fixed seed and fixed $$K$$ policy based only on full-path descriptor variance. Let medoid $$m(t)$$ be nearest to $$z(t)$$.

For each medoid, define a static chart:

$$
q_i(t)=
q_{i,m(t)}^0+
A_{i,m(t)}
\left[z(t)-z_{m(t)}\right].
$$

Avoid hard cluster jumps using descriptor-space blending:

$$
q_i(t)=
\sum_m
\frac{\exp[-\|z(t)-z_m\|^2/\tau]}
{\sum_r\exp[-\|z(t)-z_r\|^2/\tau]}
\left[
q_{i,m}^0+A_{i,m}(z(t)-z_m)
\right].
$$

No time coordinate, song section, beat, or audio feature may enter $$z$$.

**N scaling.** Add more sampled coordinates in each motif chart, never separate animated phases.

**Freeze behavior.** Repeated identical poses yield identical descriptors, blend weights, targets, and intensity.

**Targets.** Lack of motif and phrase recurrence; every moment looking like unrelated noise even when the dancer repeats a posture.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Source recurrence versus emitted-formation recurrence correlation.
- Same-snippet replay identity.
- Motif-boundary discontinuity.
- Within-motif descriptor/output distance correlation.

**Pitfall.** Clustering can become hidden canned choreography if time or audio leaks into descriptors. Explicitly test replaying the same pose at different timestamps.

## 14. Visibility-aware optics assignment

**Paper.** *Visibility-aware Trajectory Optimization with Application to Aerial Tracking* (2021), Qianhao Wang, Yuman Gao, Jialin Ji, Chao Xu, Fei Gao. arXiv:2103.06742. It uses differentiable costs for distance, view angle, and occlusion while considering dynamic feasibility.[12]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Visibility should be an explicit optimization objective, not an afterthought.

**LightSem adaptation.** Keep a body-derived nominal target but choose its visible feasible manifestation relative to fixed camera, haze, body screen projection, floor/wall, and fixture geometry.

For nominal ray $$r_i^0(t)$$, choose a spatial-amplitude parameter $$\alpha_i(t)\in$$:[0][1]

$$
r_i(t,\alpha)=
r_{i,\text{anchor}}(t)+
\alpha
\left[
r_i^0(t)-r_{i,\text{anchor}}(t)
\right].
$$

Optimize:

$$
\min_{\alpha,\theta}
\lambda_I C_{\text{intent}}
+\lambda_O C_{\text{body-occlusion}}
+\lambda_C C_{\text{ray-collision}}
+\lambda_V C_{\text{low-visibility}}
+\lambda_M C_{\text{mechanics}}.
$$

Example visibility surrogate:

$$
C_{\text{low-visibility}}=
\sum_{t,i}
\max(0,L_{\min}-L_{\text{visible}}(r_i(t)))^2.
$$

Intensity can be attenuated when a beam is invisible or redundant:

$$
a'_i(t)=a_i(t)\cdot
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
\frac{L_{\text{visible}}(r_i(t))}{L_{\text{ref}}},
0,1
\right).
$$

**N scaling.** Use sparse nearby-ray collision graphs and fixture batches. At high $$N$$, approximate interactions only among camera-neighboring projected rays.

**Freeze behavior.** Fixed body-derived nominal intent gives the same optimization problem and fixed optimum under deterministic solver settings.

**Targets.** Current 58% projected ray crossing, poor actual-hit separation, body/screen collisions, invisible but bright clutter.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Visible ray length / integrated haze contribution.
- Projected crossing rate.
- Actual hit-pair separation.
- Body overlap.
- Intent error versus optical visibility Pareto curve.
- Dimmer reduction attributable to invisibility.

**Pitfall.** It can optimize the renderer rather than choreography. Never report visibility without reporting body-intent distortion.

## 15. Constraint-aware spatial-amplitude projection

**Paper inspiration.** Visibility-aware planning jointly reasons about desired trajectory and dynamic feasibility.  Spatiotemporal robot-retargeting methods also treat constraints as part of retargeting, although their temporal refinements are forbidden here.[13][14][12]

**Borrowed mechanism.** Solve target constraints at trajectory level.

**LightSem adaptation.** Preserve source timing exactly. When mechanics cannot reproduce a gesture, shrink its spatial amplitude around a body-derived anchor instead of time-warping, phase shifting, or smoothing away the gesture.

Let upstream mapper target be $$x_i^0(t)$$, with body-derived anchor $$c_i(t)$$:

$$
x_i(t)=
c_i(t)+
\alpha_i(t)
\left[
x_i^0(t)-c_i(t)
\right],
\qquad 0\le\alpha_i(t)\le1.
$$

Solve:

$$
\max_{\alpha,\theta}
\sum_{t,i}
w_{t,i}\alpha_i(t)
-\lambda_\alpha\|D\alpha_i\|^2
$$

subject to:

$$
\theta=\operatorname{IK}(x),
$$

plus exact fixture angle, velocity, acceleration, octagonal derivative, and quantization constraints.

Set source-derived priority:

$$
w_{t,i}=
w_E E_i(t)+
w_C c_i^{\text{contact}}(t)+
w_X e_i^{\text{extremum}}(t)+
w_R r_i^{\text{recurrence}}(t).
$$

For a static interval, force:

$$
\alpha_i(t)=\alpha_i(t_0),
\qquad
\theta_i(t)=\theta_i(t_0).
$$

**N scaling.** Solve per-fixture when no relational constraints exist. For relation-based mappers, add only local neighbor separation constraints; sparse graphs keep complexity manageable.

**Freeze behavior.** Explicitly enforced constant $$\alpha$$ and $$\theta$$ during static body intervals.

**Targets.** The central observed failure: motions can have low angular error yet visibly lose gesture amplitude under limits.

**Preregistered metrics.**

- Amplitude retention $$\alpha$$ at source extrema.
- Active constraint time by type.
- Gesture onset/offset timing error.
- Raw versus emitted spectral-band RMS.
- Distribution of $$\alpha$$.
- Constant-hold leakage.

**Pitfall.** This can collapse everything toward anchors if the objective is badly weighted. Report the full $$\alpha$$ distribution, not only average angular error.

# Minimal shared API

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class BodySequence:
    xyz: NDArray[np.float64]          # [T, 22, 3], 40 Hz
    source_time_s: NDArray[np.float64]
    joint_names: tuple[str, ...]
    skeleton_edges: tuple[tuple[int, int], ...]
    root_frame: NDArray[np.float64]   # [T, 3, 3]
    provenance: dict                  # song, body hash, seed=7001

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class StageModel:
    fixtures: tuple[Fixture, ...]
    floor: Plane
    back_wall: Plane
    camera: Camera
    body_screen_geometry: Geometry
    renderer_hash: str

@dataclass
class Intent:
    target_xyz: NDArray[np.float64]   # [T, N, 3]
    intensity: NDArray[np.float64]    # [T, N]
    semantic_id: NDArray              # joint/bone/pair/descriptor provenance
    source_time_s: NDArray[np.float64]
    priority: NDArray[np.float64]     # [T, N]
    diagnostics: dict

class Mapper(Protocol):
    def extract(self, body: BodySequence) -> dict: ...
    def map_intent(
        self, features: dict, stage: StageModel, n_outputs: int
    ) -> Intent: ...

class MechanicsCompiler(Protocol):
    def solve(
        self, intent: Intent, stage: StageModel,
        limits: MechanicalLimits
    ) -> EmittedCommands: ...

class DMXEncoder(Protocol):
    def encode(
        self, emitted: EmittedCommands,
        external_rgb_bytes: NDArray[np.uint8]
    ) -> NDArray[np.uint8]: ...
```

The boundary is non-negotiable:

1. **Source**: saved body and exact timestamp provenance.
2. **Feature**: what body information is extracted.
3. **Intent**: desired targets and intensities before mechanics.
4. **Mechanics**: what motion was attenuated or altered by hardware constraints.
5. **DMX**: exact emitted bytes.
6. **Renderer**: decode only emitted bytes, never intent targets.
7. **Evaluation**: render/optics metrics separate from command metrics.

# Controls that are engineering-valid

Use fixed, declared controls instead of per-song “artistic” tuning:

- `spatial_gain`: one physical body-to-stage scale.
- `static_dimmer_floor`: test 0, a small nonzero floor, and historical 0.28 as an ablation.
- `derivative_scale`: one robust full-song quantile rule, identical across songs.
- `manifestation_width`: simultaneous spatial spread only; never temporal phase.
- `relation_set`: fixed pairs/branches/landmarks for both songs.
- `visibility_weight`: calibrated on synthetic geometry before final-song render review.
- `mechanics_tradeoff`: fixed intent-fidelity versus amplitude-retention weight.
- `N_allocation`: deterministic largest-remainder allocation from declared semantic importance.

Forbidden controls:

- Song title.
- Beat position.
- Audio-derived phase.
- Timestamp as an input feature.
- Random motion.
- Manual correction after viewing a particular song.
- Different body-feature definitions for Shpongle and Cure.

# Required evaluation

## Source-to-visible diagnostics

| Pipeline layer | Required metrics |
|---|---|
| Source body | Input hash, timestamps, units, finite values, joint-path variance |
| Features | Effective dimension, joint-response Jacobian, head/spine coverage, descriptor variance |
| Intent | Duplicate target fraction, target effective dimension, relation distortion, stage occupancy |
| Mechanics | Constraint activation, angle error, $$\alpha$$ retention, spectral attenuation, extrema timing |
| DMX | Byte hash, channel ownership, RGB byte equality, constant-command test |
| Optics | Actual-hit separation, ray crossings, body overlap, visible ray length, low-motion luminous duty |

## Causality tests

1. **Full freeze**: repeat one valid pose for all 180 seconds. Motion DMX and intensity must become constant.
2. **Inserted hold**: replace a five-second real segment with one pose. No drift is allowed in the interior.
3. **Single-joint perturbation**: perturb each joint along x/y/z and record feature, intent, emitted-angle, and rendered-hit Jacobians.
4. **Head test**: specifically perturb joint 15/head; it must cause a measurable response in candidates claiming head awareness.
5. **Nullspace test**: test the currently identified elbow perturbation direction; candidates must expose whether it is still invisible.
6. **Replay identity**: insert the identical body snippet at two timestamps. Excluding externally supplied RGB, outputs must match.
7. **N scaling**: evaluate $$N\in\{1,8,13,50,100,1000\}$$. Extra outputs must be simultaneous manifestations of source semantics.
8. **Color firewall**: assert externally supplied RGB bytes are identical before and after mapper/compiler substitution.

## Outcome families

Do not use “all command streams differ” as success. Report:

- **Body fidelity**: joint/part/relation response; extrema recall; onset/offset timing; motif recurrence.
- **Mechanics fidelity**: constraint activity; amplitude retention; emitted/raw spectral ratios.
- **Visible diversity**: hit separation; projected occupancy; crossing rate; silhouette or relational distortion.
- **Clutter**: dimmer median; low-motion brightness duty; visible-ray count; body overlap.
- **Validity**: freeze constancy; replay identity; RGB equality; deterministic hashes.

Shpongle and Cure must be reported separately. Cure’s near-static RGB1 transition is a color condition, not evidence that a motion mapper succeeded or failed. The correct comparison is body-feature-to-emitted-motion and body-feature-to-visible-optics, with color held fixed byte-for-byte.
