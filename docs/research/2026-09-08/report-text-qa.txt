 LightSem: where the research should go
 next
 Research review for Anton · 8 September 2026

 The decision
 Continue with an explicit whole-song lighting plan and a small, actively queried control space. First
 repair the evaluation materials. Keep a distributional lighting decoder as a gated research branch.

 The repository has useful audio descriptors, a working conductor and renderer, and a first preference
 pilot. Its negative results are substantial, but their scope is narrower than “audio cannot generate
 designed lighting.” The failed planner did not test explicit motif reuse; the group decoder did not
 reproduce the main mechanisms of Skip-BART or SeqLight. The comparison on page 2 explains why.

 Three research directions have the clearest connection to the actual product:

  • Remember musical returns. Give recurring sections related palettes, spatial patterns and
    movement motifs, with controlled variation. Test this directly against equally varied lighting without
    recurrence memory. MusicFrameworks motivates the representation; its lighting transfer remains a
    hypothesis.

  • Spend human attention on informative choices. Optimize three or four interpretable controls
    with active comparisons or synchronized line search. qEUBO and Sequential Line Search provide
    implementable mechanisms.

  • Preserve richer scene targets. Test colour distributions and temporally consistent fixture
    assignment before investing in a complete generative training stack. SeqLight makes this
    distinction concrete.

 A useful new warning from the literature
 The 2026 paper Glow with the Flow reports that lighting can receive strong synchronization and
 congruence ratings while viewers still find it repetitive or too literal. Its generated palettes also lose
 richness. That closely matches the failure mode LightSem needs to measure: synchronization and
 aesthetic quality are separate outcomes.

 This is a research recommendation, not evidence of a new improvement. No new lighting model,
 rendering campaign or GPU experiment was run for this review.

 Scope and reading route
 This review targets pre-rendered audio → stage lighting. Whole-song context is allowed. Dance is
 training supervision; no generated dancer is required at inference. The current Just Dance extraction
 is outside this review.

 Read pages 2–6 for the evidence, 7–9 for six discriminating experiments, and 10 for the
 implementation order. The reference notes distinguish full-paper verification from abstract-only and
 repository-only evidence. Availability was checked as of the report date.

 The 39 ratings are a pilot
 All 39 ratings come from Anton across 29 registered song groups. Grouped held-out accuracy is
 56.4% for music fit and 59.0% for designed appearance; both conditional uncertainty intervals
 include chance. The first equals its majority-side accuracy baseline. Appearance log loss is 0.739,
 worse than the uninformative 0.693. These measure preference prediction, not a generated show
 winning against the default. Ratings audit

 Saved-video image luma differs by a median 16.1% within pairs, despite a 0.67% median difference

 confirmed mismatch, although it does not explain every difference. Image luma is not calibrated
 physical luminance.

 Keep the original pilot immutable. The H-L confirmation gate remains unmet, and a fresh
 fitted-versus-default preference evaluation has not happened. Canonical audio identities also need
 checking: matching titles alone do not prove duplicate recordings.

 Direct lighting research: useful
 distinctions
 Skip-BART · existing paper, worth revisiting accurately
 Zhao et al., 2025 / ICLR 2026 repository designation. The method combines an offline music
 encoder with autoregressive discrete hue/value generation, aligned audio/light embedding skips,
 PianoBART transfer and constrained sampling. Its RPMC labels come from videos: 699 samples from
 35 performances, concentrated in rock, punk, metal and core. These are camera-derived colours, not
 console-authored DMX. Full paper

 The official repository links weights and processed data. Model and dataset cards declare Apache 2.0
 and CC BY 4.0 respectively; a separate repository license was not located. Its primary-colour output
 and fixed saturation limit direct transfer. A nonsignificant difference from humans does not establish
 equivalence.

 SeqLight · existing paper, a materially different decoder
 Zhao et al., May 2026 preprint. SeqLight predicts full hue/value histograms with KL loss, then
 decomposes them into individual lights using a goal-conditioned policy. HER-generated
 demonstrations, behavior cloning, AIRL and GRPO replace professional per-fixture demonstrations;
 temporal constraints discourage discontinuities. Full paper

 The study covers 30 participants and six pieces, including three generated out-of-domain songs. That
 is limited generalization evidence. Code exists, but trained weights and a repository license were not
 verified; the README remains incomplete. Start with the representation and assignment questions,
 not an assumed ready-to-run full checkpoint.

 Glow with the Flow · new, close to the renderer problem
 Robinson et al., PacificVis Notes 2026. Pretrained audio analysis and source-video palettes feed
 rules that generate sustained layers, spatial modulation and discrete events in editable light objects. It
 is not a newly trained audio-to-light generator. Its 32-person study used 15 contexts from three videos
 in an automotive visualization; corrected nonsignificant tests establish neither equivalence nor
 authoring time savings. Code/data were not located. Full paper

 Borrow the editable representation and separation of slow layers from fast events. Audio-only
 inference needs an independently supplied palette.

 Two leads with unresolved assets
 AuraLuxMuse / LightMuse: the source repository handles fixture attributes and cue retrieval, and
 contains generated grandMA2 XML. Those files are model outputs, not verified authored training
 examples. No primary paper, source training corpus, weights or license was verified. Treat it as a code
 reference for cue representations.

 LightingGen: Wang et al., IEEE TMM 2026, is a verified publication, LightingGen: A DMX Based
 Generation Method for Entertainment Stage Lighting. Publisher DOI and publisher-deposited
 metadata establish the record. Full methods and code/data were inaccessible or not located. Dataset
 hours and named components appearing only in secondary summaries are excluded here.

 Whole-song structure: remember, then
 vary
 The representation is the important new experiment
 lightsem/audio/structure.py already computes recurrence-based clustering, but uses it to produce
 boundaries. Its later labels are energy heuristics. The generator does not retain section identity to
 recall a lighting motif. train_planner.py instead regressed per-bar controls. An explicit recurrence
 experiment therefore changes the mechanism.

 A proposed intermediate representation is:

     Audio timeline → section identity + position within section → motif identity +
     controlled variation → continuous fixture trajectories → DMX

 For example, A–B–A′ could recall a related palette and wave pattern when A returns, while changing
 density or phase. This is a proposed lighting design, not a learned correspondence established by a
 paper. Keep colour diversity and motif identity separate: more frequent random colours need not
 produce more coherent variety.

 Papers that make the distinction useful
 MusicFrameworks — Dai et al., ISMIR 2021. A hierarchical symbolic melody representation
 separates structural repetition and rhythmic/melodic controls. The transferable idea is to represent
 reuse explicitly. It does not establish lighting performance; an official implementation was not located
 in the bounded search. Author PDF

 Whole-Song Hierarchical Generation — Wang, Min and Xia, ICLR 2024. Four levels connect
 global form to local symbolic accompaniment, using cascaded diffusion and external controls. The
 paper links code/checkpoints, but their contents and terms were not audited. This supports a
 hierarchy as an architectural precedent; it does not guarantee literal chorus-motif reuse or justify
 porting a music diffusion model. Proceedings and paper

 Repetition-Structure Inference with Formal Prototypes — Finkensiep et al., ISMIR 2023.
 Formal templates constrain a repetition grammar. The proof of concept studies 298 melodies of 8–24
 notes; it is not an audio segmenter. Its Julia code uses an ILP solver, defaulting to Gurobi. Exact
 inference is expensive. Borrow the interpretation of repeated sections before considering this
 specialized implementation. Paper

 Analysis front ends already in the vault
 SongFormer — Hao et al., 2025; revised April 2026. Short/long-context music features and
 heterogeneous supervision support boundary and function prediction. The official repository has
 training/inference code and checkpoint links; code is CC BY 4.0, while checkpoint terms require
 separate review. Its pop-oriented taxonomy has no dedicated EDM drop class. Profile resources before
 scheduling it on the 12 GB GPU. Full paper

 EDMFormer — Sajeer et al., March 2026 preprint. EDM-specific adaptation and the 98-track
 EDM-98 corpus are relevant, but the small, singly annotated corpus limits conclusions. An actual
 code/data download was not located. Keep this as a watch item, not an immediate dependency. Full
 paper

 Learn from fewer, better human
 decisions
 Start with a small, observable control space
 The immediate task is choosing settings Anton likes across songs. It does not require a neural reward
 model. Freeze brightness initially and expose three or four controls with clear visual effects: wave
 phase spread, motion rate, colour-transition duration and colour-change frequency. The existing 39
 ratings cannot supervise controls that were absent from their renders.

 Sequential Line Search — Koyama et al., SIGGRAPH 2017. Bayesian optimization proposes a
 line through a larger design space; people select a preferred point. Official MIT C++/Python code
 makes it an approachable starting point. A synchronized loop and slider may suit time-varying
 lighting, but that adaptation is untested. A line-search task is not equivalent to one binary vote.
 Project

 qEUBO — Astudillo et al., AISTATS 2023. Select comparisons by expected utility of their best
 option. Its optimality statements have assumptions; they do not promise sample efficiency on this
 project. The current BoTorch tutorial supplies PairwiseGP and analytic EUBO, with CPU operation and
 MIT library licensing. This is the most direct active-pair baseline. Paper record

 BOPE — Lin et al., AISTATS 2022. Separate parameters → measurable outcomes from outcomes →
 human utility. This could connect candidate selection to useful renderer diagnostics. However, a few
 scalar summaries may omit what matters in a video. Original MIT replication code is archived; a
 maintained official tutorial remains available. Paper

 Useful extensions, with narrower evidence
 Sequential Gallery — Koyama, Sato and Goto, SIGGRAPH 2020. Plane-search galleries have MIT
 code. A preliminary six-person study averaged 5.36 subtasks to satisfaction. Each subtask involved a
 gallery, not a single A/B judgement; viewing temporal variants may be much slower. Compare quality
 per minute, not nominal task count. Author paper

 Constrained PBO — Iwai et al., IJCAI 2025. Preference acquisition incorporates feasibility. Eleven
 professional designers performed tasks with 50 comparisons in 6D/12D spaces; the constraint was
 predicted CTR, not deployed CTR. No official code release was verified. LightSem can initially reject
 measured luminance or clipping violations directly. Proceedings paper

 Consecutive PBO — Erarslan et al., November 2025 preprint. Candidate reuse and explicit
 indifference are attractive. Full acquisition is costly: a reported 6D synthetic case took 345±34
 seconds per iteration. A downloadable official code archive was not verified. Borrow ties and reuse
 before the full optimizer. Full paper

 Preferential Multi-Objective BO — Astudillo et al., 2024/2025. Separate objective-specific
 choices can explore trade-offs, but the inspected version evaluates synthetic/simulated tasks and no
 code was verified. Since the two local questions agree on 33/39 pairs, a full Pareto-search system is
 premature. Readable paper

 Timing, metrics and usable data
 Beat tracking is a bounded benchmark opportunity
 Beat This! — Foscarin, Schlüter and Widmer, ISMIR 2024. The transformer predicts
 beats/downbeats without mandatory DBN postprocessing. Strong F1 results coexist with weaker
 continuity measures in the paper. The official repository provides MIT code/weights, a smaller model
 and CPU inference. It explicitly warns about overlap with pretrained training data. Full paper

 LightSem's audited constant-grid fit matches mapper BPM exactly on only about 62–65% of the
 checked material; octave and 2:3 alternatives matter. Benchmark a replacement against
 independently verified beat/downbeat timing and continuity. A higher Light-BAS score alone cannot
 establish that the grid improved. Mapper BPM is also a reference requiring validation, not
 unquestionable musical ground truth. Local audit

 Measure several kinds of correspondence
 Cross-Modal Metrics — Kohl, Wursthorn and Weiß, ACM Multimedia 2025. The publisher
 abstract distinguishes beat/change concurrence, loudness/brightness correlation and musical/lighting
 structural correspondence using control parameters. It reports distinguishing real shows from random
 sequences and examining four styles. Full formulas, implementation and public data were not verified
 because the full paper was inaccessible. Publisher

 The project-specific implication is to keep a diagnostic vector: timing, simultaneous fixture activation,
 spatial motion, colour occupancy/change, section contrast and recurrence. These proposed
 diagnostics still need validation against known failure cases and human responses. No new composite
 aesthetic score is justified.

 A useful negative-control set includes a constant scene, synchronized whole-rig flashing, a travelling
 wave, time-shifted events and section-shuffled motifs. A metric that ranks every synchronized flash
 above a preferred wave should expose its limitation instead of becoming the sole optimization target.

 Raveform is already a practical asset
 Kim et al., TISMIR 2026. Raveform includes 1,423 tracks with expert tempo, beat/downbeat and
 functional-structure annotations, with an eight-fold evaluation schema. Its collection supplies track
 links and annotations; it does not establish redistribution rights for the music or provide lighting
 controls. Dataset-specific licensing remains an asset-check gap. Publication

 The local annotation bundle already exists under /mnt/lightsem-data/raveform/raveform. Check actual
 audio availability and canonical identity before using a frozen held-out subset. This can support
 structure and beat evaluation without new lighting labels.

 What additional Just Dance processing changes
 More processed videos may improve coverage and target reliability. They do not automatically fix a
 collapsed target representation or validate a conductor. After extraction and downstream feature joins
 finish, freeze the manifest, deduplicate song identities, measure usable duration and target quality,
 and assess which descriptor gaps the added material addresses. Training on the new corpus belongs
 to a separate experiment.

 Six experiments that can change the
 decision
 All budgets and thresholds below are proposed design choices. They have not been
 registered or run.

 1. Repair the comparison materials and validate diagnostics
 Question: can a fresh pair differ in its intended treatment without unintended brightness or rendering
 changes?

 Correct the blinder metric/renderer mismatch in a versioned metric. Verify it with analytically known
 dimmer sweeps. Reprocess saved videos on CPU, preserve historical scores, and measure new
 candidate renders with a fixed camera, exposure and image region.

 For non-brightness experiments, choose a prospective image-luma tolerance, initially 3%, and also
 inspect clipping and distributions. This is an engineering starting point, not a perceptual equality
 standard. A brightness treatment must remain a separate study; normalizing it away would erase the
 intervention.

 Gate: pass controlled renderer checks and material matching before new aesthetic comparisons.
 Probe the proposed timing/spatial/colour metrics with the negative controls on page 6. Metric validity
 is distinct from whether any candidate looks better. Existing-video analysis needs no GPU; newly
 constructed controls require rendering.

 2. Benchmark beat/downbeat analysis before changing it
 Question: does Beat This improve the actual timing reference used by the conductor?

 Freeze 20 development and 40 evaluation songs with independent beat/downbeat annotations and
 verified identities, using locally available Raveform material where suitable. Audit pretrained overlap
 first. Compare the current grid fitter, frozen Beat This outputs, and the existing analysis baseline at
 the same offline context budget. Tune only on development songs.

 Primary: song-macro beat F1; separately report downbeat F1, continuity, octave/2:3 errors and
 runtime. As a provisional advancement threshold, seek at least +5 percentage points beat F1 with an
 interval above zero, and no material continuity/downbeat deterioration under predeclared margins.
 Forty songs may leave small effects unresolved.

 Interpretation: an improved timing benchmark earns a conductor ablation, not an aesthetic
 improvement claim. CPU inference is supported; local throughput has not been measured.

 Experiments · recurrence and
 preferences
 3. Isolate recurrence memory from ordinary variation
 Question: does recalling a motif when musical material returns improve whole-song design?

 Freeze 12 development and 20 evaluation songs. Compare current composition, a varied section
 controller without recurrence memory, and the same controller with recurrence-linked motifs. Match
 palette inventory, change count and average image luma across the latter two arms. Use identical
 descriptors, timing, rig and seeds.

 Start with manually checked section identities to test the representation; then replace them with
 automatic identities. Show enough of both section occurrences to judge a return. Arbitrary isolated
 15-second clips cannot test this hypothesis.

 Primary: held-out designed-appearance preference; secondary music fit, perceived repetition and
 recurrence consistency. Use 40 locked comparisons, two contrasts per song, with song-level
 uncertainty. A provisional 65% win rate and an interval above chance justify further confirmation; a
 point estimate alone does not.

 Null: recurrence adds no benefit over equally varied composition. This inexpensive representation
 test should precede a new full-song neural planner.

 4. Compare active preference acquisition with random sampling
 Question: at an equal number of pair queries, does EUBO choose a better setting than random pairs?
 Viewing time is a separate measured cost; this design does not equalize human effort.

 Freeze three or four observable wave/colour controls and brightness. Give EUBO and Sobol/random
 acquisition 20 newly rated training pairs each, with the same development-song schedule, candidate
 bounds and rendering budget. Use a declared low-dimensional utility model; do not silently add a
 large song embedding to a 40-pair study.

 Freeze the two recommended settings. On 20 unused songs, compare the active recommendation
 with both the current default and the random-acquisition recommendation: 40 locked pairs. Add eight
 side-swapped repeat or identical-render checks. Total: 88 pair presentations, with response counts
 and viewing time recorded separately.

 Primary: designed-appearance win rate by song, with music fit as a separate gate. Target 65% wins
 and a confidence bound above chance for each prespecified comparison before claiming success;
 account for testing both contrasts. This small pilot detects only large gains.

 Record “no meaningful preference” and “cannot judge” separately. For a simple binary model, ties
 can initially be abstentions; report their frequency and repeat stability. A later tie-likelihood study
 must compare calibrated alternatives. Fitting/acquisition can use CPU; new videos and human
 decisions are still required.

 Experiments · scene representation and
 authored looks
 5. Separate scene representation from fixture assignment
 Question: does the six-moment target limit scene reconstruction, and does fixture assignment limit
 temporal consistency? These oracle contrasts cannot explain training collapse by themselves.

 First run an oracle rendering check with real target histograms. Compare six-moment reconstruction
 versus H/V distribution preservation, crossed with independent-frame versus temporally constrained
 fixture assignment. Freeze the scene source, fixture geometry and intensity scale.

 Primary diagnostics: distribution reconstruction, colour occupancy, frame-to-frame fixture changes
 and persistence. Use held-out human comparisons to assess whether any reconstruction gain survives
 rendering. Define reconstruction and continuity tolerances on development data; do not choose them
 after inspecting evaluation results.

 Only if the oracle check passes should a small audio-conditioned experiment compare the incumbent
 with a histogram-based target, under identical data splits, capacity budget and rollout evaluation.
 Reserve the GPU until extraction finishes. A faithful Skip-BART baseline is a separate implementation
 task; do not label this simplified ablation a SeqLight reproduction.

 Null: a richer target changes statistics without improving visible design. In that case, stop before the
 costly RL/decomposition stack.

 6. Test an authored-look ceiling before collecting a corpus
 Question: can the current renderer produce the kind of lighting Anton wants when timing and motif
 choices are supplied well?

 Build a compact, original bank of looks with documented palette, movement, spatial spread and
 transition controls. On development songs, supply a manually reviewed cue timeline; on 20 held-out
 songs compare a similarly specified oracle plan with the current automatic plan. Keep renderer
 capability and timing sources explicit.

 Interpretation: if an independently reviewed reference plan is faithfully rendered yet judged poor,
 inspect renderer primitives. Otherwise, an unsuccessful manual plan is inconclusive. If the oracle wins,
 the planner/selection gap becomes concrete. This does not establish that audio can learn the oracle
 policy.

 A cue-retrieval baseline can follow using separately authored development examples. The
 AuraLuxMuse source is a representation reference; its generated XML is not a substitute for an
 authored corpus. This experiment requires new authoring and rating work, even if it requires no neural
 training.

 Recommended continuation and limits
 Order of work
 First, make the next comparison trustworthy. Version the brightness diagnostic, verify canonical
 song identities, and freeze a clean evaluation manifest. Keep the original 39 ratings and videos as
 historical pilot evidence.

 Then test the two nearest product changes. Run the recurrence-memory experiment and a small
 active preference study as separate interventions. Freeze descriptors, beat analysis and renderer
 within each comparison.

 Run the timing benchmark alongside CPU work. A clean timing win can later enter its own
 rendering ablation. Newly extracted Just Dance material should enter training only after its manifest
 and downstream features are complete and audited.

 Escalate conditionally. A successful renderer oracle earns planner work. A successful histogram
 oracle earns a controlled decoder study. Neither success automatically justifies implementing an
 entire diffusion or RL pipeline.

 What this search changed
 The strongest additions to the existing vault are the human-in-the-loop optimization methods, editable
 light-object design, formal recurrence representations, Beat This and the control-parameter metric
 paper. Skip-BART, SeqLight, SongFormer and EDMFormer were already present; the contribution here
 is correcting their relationship to the local code and checking availability.

 Fourteen new paper records were selected for the project vault. LightingGen and AuraLuxMuse remain
 documented leads with incomplete evidence. This review does not establish a new openly usable
 authored DMX corpus.

 Evidence limits and source reconciliation
 Perplexity was used successfully for discovery, followed by primary papers, official repositories/model
 cards and local code. Its summary overstated SeqLight weight availability and blurred hierarchical
 generation with guaranteed motif reuse. Those claims were corrected. It also resurfaced Witek's
 groove paper, already in the vault; that was not counted as a new discovery.

 Direct studies use few songs, camera-derived targets, narrow genres or ambient environments.
 Preference optimization has not established transfer to dynamic lighting. Several works are preprints.
 “Not located” describes this search, not proof that an asset does not exist.

 Local metrics come from the continuation and ratings audits. Existing validation material has
 identity/split caveats, so old results should not be promoted into new locked-test evidence. The
 ratings cover one rater and older controls. More observations, more questions and repeated views do
 not automatically create more independent songs.

 Searches covered direct lighting/DMX, musical form, active preferences, beat tracking and EDM
 annotations. Follow-ups resolved availability contradictions. Discovery stopped when mechanisms had
 primary support or bounded evidence gaps and further searching was unlikely to change the
 experiment order.

 Reading priority: Glow with the Flow → Sequential Line Search / qEUBO → MusicFrameworks →
 SeqLight's representation and decomposition sections → Beat This. Use the remaining papers to refine
 a successful pilot rather than expanding the first implementation.
