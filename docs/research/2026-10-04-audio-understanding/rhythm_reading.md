# Source-aware rhythm and transcription: reading notes

Research date: 2026-10-04. Research only: no LightSem training, inference comparison, render or benchmark was run. Paper scores below are author-reported and do not establish transfer to lighting. Historical failures should remain scoped to the exact mechanism, input, targets and evaluation used; they do not exclude a method family.

## What this literature supplies

The strongest immediate contribution is **time-resolved, source-indexed evidence**: kick/snare/hat onsets, bass activity and attacks, pitched-instrument activity, vocal entrances and rests. Such evidence can be used as cached teacher outputs, direct diagnostic inputs, or supervised auxiliary targets for a compact audio encoder. A single beat track or song embedding does not supply these distinctions.

Four especially relevant implementations were examined in depth through the arXiv MCP: **YourMT3+**, **MuScriptor**, **Inverse Drum Machine**, and **ADT_STR**. Two additional primary manuscripts, **MIAMT-JEPA (2025)** and **Reconstruction Meets Prediction (2026)**, were read from the authors' university site because no arXiv ID was verified. The latter directly supports revisiting reconstruction and predictive latent learning with a changed training schedule.

None of these papers proves general understanding of polyrhythm, swing, call-and-response, or source interaction in produced electronic/pop music. Most evaluate transcription at a 50 ms onset tolerance, which can conceal errors important for microtiming. None of the mono systems below supplies spatial source position; retain the original stereo/multichannel signal for a separate spatial path.

For experiments, distinguish syncopation (accent/onset displacement relative to a metrical reference), swing (unequal subdivision timing), tempo change, simultaneous rhythmic periodicities and polymeter. A single mixed drum **stem/file** may contain multiple event streams and polyrhythm; file count is not the relevant unit. Likewise, a rendered MIDI note-on is a synthesis control timestamp, which can precede the audible attack depending on sample envelope/latency. Calibrate that offset before calling MIDI timing exact acoustic-onset ground truth, especially for 10–20 ms metrics.

## 1. YourMT3+: reproducible source-indexed note teacher and partial-label design

Chang, Benetos, Kirchhoff and Dixon, 2024. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04822), [official repository](https://github.com/mimbres/YourMT3), [official checkpoint host](https://huggingface.co/mimbres/YourMT3).

**Mechanism.** A 2.048-second mono 16 kHz audio crop becomes spectral features. The strongest variant replaces the ordinary MT3 encoder with a Perceiver time-frequency hierarchy: spectral cross-attention, latent attention and temporal attention. A mixture of experts selects two of eight experts. Twenty-six latents are grouped into thirteen instrument channels; each channel uses a shared-parameter autoregressive decoder. This makes the loss for unannotated instruments maskable. The model uses note/instrument/time/on-off tokens, with vocal melody and chorus classes; it does **not** provide calibrated continuous performance dynamics, since unused velocity tokens are removed.

**Data mechanism worth reproducing.** Intra-song augmentation drops stems; cross-dataset augmentation combines stems from different examples, mixes audio and merges labels. This is especially useful for incomplete labels, where absence of annotation must not be interpreted as absence of sound. The paper uses ten training datasets spanning synthetic mixtures, classical ensembles, piano, guitar, drums and singing.

**Evidence.** Table 3 reports Slakh instrument-agnostic onset F1 84.6 and offset F1 70.7 for the full model, against the authors' MT3 Colab run at 75.2 and 56.8. Their cumulative ablation gives substantial gains from stem augmentation and data balancing. These are transcription metrics on a synthetic benchmark, not general source-understanding or lighting metrics.

**Failure boundaries.** On RWC commercial pop, non-main instruments other than piano/bass/vocals/drums often score below 10%; the authors identify synthetic-to-real timbre mismatch and octave errors. Singing onsets often miss the 50 ms tolerance but fall within 100 ms. The short crop and independent channel decoder are not a model of phrase development. Cross-song stem mixing increases timbral coverage but can destroy genuine groove/harmony relationships, so it should not be the only training distribution for an interaction latent.

**LightSem use.** Cache instrument activity, pitch/chroma, onset time and confidence, with errors audited on actual target genres. More attractive as an offline teacher and an example of partial-label loss masking than as a mandatory reconstruction bottleneck. A student could predict dense per-source activity/onset targets while keeping its own continuous acoustic residual features. Do not label missing instruments silent merely because the teacher emits no notes.

**Availability/license detail.** Official GitHub landing repository declares GPL-3.0 and links a pre-release code route and demos. Its checkpoint repository labels weights Apache-2.0. These are separate artifacts; do not propagate one license to the other or assume the short GitHub landing tree is the complete training package. Dataset setup code does not grant all constituent audio licenses. Runtime/install/checkpoint execution was not tested.

## 2. MuScriptor: current real-mixture teacher, not an affordable from-scratch reproduction

Rouard, Krause, Roebel, Simon-Gabriel and Défossez, 2026. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08168), [official code](https://github.com/muscriptor/muscriptor), [official large weights](https://huggingface.co/MuScriptor/muscriptor-large).

**Mechanism.** Decoder-only transformer receives five seconds of mono 16 kHz audio as a 100 Hz, 512-bin mel spectrogram and optional track-level instrument-presence tokens. It predicts MT3-style note events for 36 grouped instrument classes. Synthetic pretraining uses 1.45 million MIDI files, more than 250 soundfonts, randomized instruments/velocities/tempo/pitch and detuning. Real training uses an internal 170,000-recording, approximately 11,000-hour corpus with audio-symbolic alignment. A 300-song verified subset supports policy-gradient post-training with note/transcription F-scores as reward.

**Evidence and qualification.** On their 372-song internal test set, the complete large model reports Multi F1 48.2 versus YourMT3+ 21.9; Drum F1 50.2 versus 41.4. Most headline experiments provide **ground-truth track instrument presence** as conditioning, which LightSem will not automatically know. Public benchmark results are mixed: on RWC-Pop Multi F1 is 25.6 versus 19.1, while on RWC-Classical it is 36.0 versus 40.5. This is a strong candidate teacher with substantial remaining errors, not a solved source recognizer.

**Important ablations.** Synthetic-only training remains weak; real-data fine-tuning matters greatly. On the paper's 300M transcription comparison, mel input beats Encodec and MERT input for most aggregate note metrics. That is evidence about this model/task/input interface, not proof that all learned acoustic latents are unsuitable. Tokenization cannot represent simultaneous overlapping same-pitch notes from the same instrument group: restoring those reference notes lowers Multi F1 from 48.2 to 42.0. Five-second crops and instrument conditioning improve local consistency but do not directly evaluate phrase-level interaction.

**LightSem use.** Evaluate its unconditioned or independently predicted-instrument mode on short, annotated target-domain clips; use disagreement with YourMT3+/specialist drums to identify unreliable labels. It can supply pseudo-labels and confidence-weighted dense supervision, but the internal corpus is not an available LightSem training dataset. A compact student must be measured against verified target labels, not only agreement with MuScriptor.

**Availability/license.** Official code is MIT; released weights are CC BY-NC 4.0. Repository currently lists small 103M, medium 307M and large approximately 1.4B artifacts, while paper nominal sizes are 100M/300M/1.3B. Preserve exact checkpoint/version in any future run. Non-commercial weight terms are material when choosing a deployable teacher/student pipeline; no legal conclusion about derived labels is implied here. Internal real corpus and curated RL/test subsets were not found released.

## 3. Inverse Drum Machine: compact factorization and reconstruction as supervision

Torres, Peeters and Richard, arXiv 2025; official repository describes TASLP 2026 publication. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03337), [code and pretrained artifact](https://github.com/bernardo-torres/inverse-drum-machine), [license](https://github.com/bernardo-torres/inverse-drum-machine/blob/main/LICENSE).

**Mechanism.** A ConvNeXt acoustic encoder produces 32-dimensional frame features. Heads predict per-class onset activity, hit volume, overall track gains and a drum-kit embedding. An instrument-conditioned TCN generates one-shot drum sounds; convolution with onset/volume streams reconstructs each drum source and their sum. Training uses drum-mixture reconstruction (multi-resolution STFT), supervised onsets (BCE), and kit classification (cross entropy). It does not need isolated ground-truth stems for the training loss, but **it does require onset annotations and kit labels in the demonstrated experiment**. Ground-truth onsets are passed into synthesis during training, so reconstruction gradients do not train the onset branch directly.

**Scope.** This is drums-only transcription/separation (DTD), not arbitrary full-mix drum transcription (DTM). StemGMD provides approximately 136 hours, nine classes and ten synthesized kits. Six kits train the model; four are held out. The model has 542.1k parameters versus the 49.1M LarsNet baseline. The authors explicitly avoid claiming transcription SOTA from this controlled experiment.

**Evidence.** Direct synthesis gives useful spectral reconstruction and better silence behavior than the supervised baseline; masked separation is somewhat weaker than stereo LarsNet. Held-out-kit precision declines. The distinction between open and closed hats is meaningful: their reported masked nSDR values differ markedly (6.68 versus 1.66 dB), illustrating why coarse class averages obscure source-specific failure.

**Limitations important for the proposed latent.** The mixture embedding is a **supervised six-kit classifier**, not an established universal disentangled timbre latent. One-second samples cannot fully model long cymbal decays. Volume is not an established perceptually accurate MIDI velocity/timbre control. The rendering assumption is best suited to repeated one-shots; rolls, sample modulation, nonstationary effects, hand percussion and dense production need new validation.

**LightSem use.** Reimplement the compact factorization and onset/activity heads, perhaps using reconstruction only as an auxiliary constraint. Its explicit time-of-hit versus timbre/volume distinction is relevant to separating rhythm from color-related texture. Use real annotations or audited teachers for onsets. An acoustic reconstruction module here is a training target, not an autonomous lamp-motion controller; it does not authorize inserting generated movement when body transfer fails.

**Availability.** Apache-2.0 repository, pretrained directory and training/inference code verified. README says evaluation code is still forthcoming. StemGMD and GMD are separate resources whose exact audio/sample licenses must be tracked when materializing a dataset; the model repository license does not cover all upstream sounds.

## 4. ADT_STR: revisit synthetic drum training with stronger timbre diversity

Melucci, Merialdo and Akama, 2026. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09520), [official code](https://github.com/pier-maker92/ADT_STR), [official model hub](https://huggingface.co/Pierfrancesco/adt-str).

**Mechanism.** Manually label 1,421 one-shots in 26 percussion classes; use the CLAP audio encoder to compute class prototypes and assign the other 7,074 sounds by cosine similarity. The complete 8,495-sample pool renders random 2.56-second crops from Lakh MIDI matched files, with within-class waveform interpolation. A seq2seq transformer learns timestamp/class/optional velocity tokens. CLAP's role is **sample curation**, not a claim that a pooled CLAP vector resolves musical timing.

**Evidence.** Paper Table 2 reports aggregate F1 0.73 on ENST and 0.79 on MDB for the full setting, compared with 0.67/0.73 from the manually labelled one-shot subset. Name-based sample labels give 0.70/0.76. These results support diversity and label quality as testable variables.

**Boundary.** Evaluation is explicitly **drums only**, even though source datasets can include accompaniment. Twenty-six classes are mapped down to the evaluation taxonomy, so there is no demonstrated independent 26-class accuracy guarantee. Samples come from twelve public packs, with a seed subset link; publicly downloadable is not itself a uniform training/redistribution license. Timbre augmentation does not demonstrate preserved or recognized swing, polyrhythm, phrase development, or success on Shpongle/Cure For Me. The paper's word “tempo” in its token description refers to quantized temporal tokens, not evidence of a separate tempo-understanding head.

**LightSem use.** A concrete experiment could compare a common student architecture under weak soundfont, diverse one-shot and real/pseudo-labelled drum data, with the same rhythm patterns/splits. Hold out kits, samples and MIDI families, and test stems plus full mixes. Preserve human timing/velocity when available; do not quantize all training examples to a fixed beat grid. Treat cosine similarity as a score requiring calibration, not a probability of label correctness.

**Availability.** Official code states CC BY-SA 4.0. GitHub README has a placeholder Hugging Face example, but its actual model-card link resolves to `Pierfrancesco/adt-str`; the public HF API lists `model.safetensors` and tau-0.4/0.6/0.8 checkpoint files. Artifact existence was verified; weights were not downloaded or executed. HF API metadata did not expose a separate weight license, so retain the repository statement and mark precise checkpoint licensing for artifact-level verification.

## 5. MIAMT-JEPA and reconstruction→prediction: a particularly relevant revisit

The [2025 primary paper](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2025/PilatakiEtAl-WASPAA2025.pdf) uses masked time-frequency prediction with an EMA target encoder, approximately 159 hours of unlabelled pretraining, and eleven pitched instrument groups **excluding voice and drums**. A shallow frozen-encoder transcriber outperforms its joint JEPA-plus-transcription fine-tuning on the reported datasets. For example, Slakh instrument-aware F1 is 45.1 for the frozen probe versus 27.9 for joint fine-tuning. Latent clustering favors timbre/register more than chroma. This makes optimization schedule and frozen versus trainable representation a meaningful experimental variable. Code/configurations are available under GPL-3.0 at [amt-jepa](https://github.com/marypilataki/amt-jepa); released pretrained weights were not verified.

The [2026 follow-up](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2026/PilatakiEtAl-SPL2026-preprint.pdf) changes that schedule: use only transcription loss during supervised fine-tuning. MAE→JEPA initialization reaches URMP F1 78.2 versus MAE 74.2 and JEPA 72.0. Effective rank alone does not predict this gain. The reverse JEPA→MAE sequence collapses in their configuration (RankMe 79), a scoped negative result. The study excludes drum understanding and removes notes shorter than 130 ms during post-processing, limiting its suitability as a fast-rhythm teacher. The [live official code README](https://github.com/marypilataki/amt-mae) describes ViT-B/AudioMAE and CC BY-NC 4.0 code; a cached repository summary still says 2025, while the live citation is 2026. Pretrained weights were not verified.

**Proposed revisit, not a paper finding:** compare supervised-only, MAE pretraining, JEPA pretraining, and MAE→JEPA using the same compact audio encoder, same audio/splits and dense source/rhythm labels; separately test frozen and transcription-only fine-tuning. Keep a joint-loss variant as a diagnostic, not a presumed winner. Probe onsets, held-out timbres, silence and interventions alongside rank/variance; never select solely on an attractive latent visualization or clustering metric.

## Proposed compact representation and the tests that make it meaningful

These are research design proposals, not established findings from the cited papers.

1. **Source tracks at a common timebase.** Emit probabilities for percussion subclasses plus bass/vocal/pitched-family activity, onset confidence, local energy/attack strength and uncertainty. Start with coarser labels where teachers are reliable; retain an unknown/unassigned channel. Store raw logits/activations as well as event lists so threshold inactivity can be separated from representation collapse.
2. **Two timescales.** Keep approximately 10–20 ms local targets for attacks/microtiming and a slower bar/phrase context. Local student features can be compressed downstream without destroying the training signal. Beat/downbeat estimates give an analysis coordinate system derived from audio, not an independent motion oscillator.
3. **Interaction targets.** Derive, with uncertainty, beat-relative onset phase, inter-onset-interval distributions, pairwise onset coincidence/lag, source alternation, density/accent trajectories, and phrase-level repeats/holds. Mask undefined targets during silence. Preserve separate subdivision hypotheses rather than force all patterns into one metrical grid. Compare same-BPM tracks with different grooves.
4. **Controlled interventions.** Mute one real stem, shift only the snare, alter hat subdivision, change timbre while preserving MIDI timing, change dynamics while preserving onsets, and remix pan while preserving mono rhythm. Measure locality of response: the assigned representation should change; unaffected sources should remain stable. Use actual source stems or licensed synthetic ground truth, not informal auditory assumptions.
5. **Avoid teacher agreement as the only score.** Manually verify a small target-domain set, include electronic/nonstandard percussion and dense full mixes, and report precision/recall/timing error by class and by active versus silent intervals. Evaluate ±10/20/50 ms tolerances where annotation precision supports them. Full-mix versus isolated-drum performance exposes separator-induced errors.
6. **Separate failure levels.** Representation variance/rank, decoding inactivity, class confusion, saturation, duplicated channels, and intentionally absent sources are different diagnostics. Validate input dependence before comparing architectures. Test silence/frozen audio and tempo-aligned different songs; nonidentical hashes alone are insufficient.
7. **LightSem connection remains gated.** First qualify the audio representation. Then hold the accepted body source, mapper, color baseline and rendering settings fixed while testing a defined representation change. Later demos must use each song's own trajectory for both Shpongle and AURORA—Cure For Me. Audio improvements cannot justify substituting procedural autonomous motion or bypassing frozen-body and limb-assignment sensitivity checks.

## Practical next investigation order

Run a small frozen-teacher audit before committing to new model training: source-indexed YourMT3+/MuScriptor predictions, ADTOF full-mixture drums, and ADT_STR on both verified drum stems and the original mixture. Their disagreement and the hand-checked errors determine which labels are safe to distill. In parallel, reproduce the compact source/onset heads and a matched-budget MAE/JEPA schedule comparison. IDM supplies a low-parameter reconstructive factorization worth testing with more diverse data. Do not make expensive from-scratch reproduction of MuScriptor's unavailable real corpus a prerequisite.

This order is a feasibility recommendation, not a permanent exclusion of any family. A previously unsuccessful objective can earn a new test when the causal mechanism changes—for example dense source targets instead of pooled similarity, real-mixture labels instead of synthetic-only supervision, or transcription-only fine-tuning instead of simultaneous competing losses.
